# AI tells: the full catalogue

Use this as a checklist when scanning an English draft (Indonesian: `indonesian-tells.md`). In academic and proposal text, the fixes here are tempered by `academic-and-proposals.md`: no contractions or fragments, keep calibrated hedges, never touch citations or numbers.

Use this as a checklist when scanning a draft. Each section says what the tell is, why it reads as machine-written, and what to do instead. Structure tells (sections 1-4) matter most; vocabulary (section 5) is the easiest to spot but the least important to fix on its own.

## 1. Openers and closers

AI front-loads context and back-loads a summary. Humans start in the middle and stop when done.

**Openers to cut**
- "In today's fast-paced / digital / ever-evolving world..."
- "In the realm of...", "When it comes to...", "In an era where..."
- "Have you ever wondered...?"
- "Let's dive in", "Let's explore", "Let's break it down"
- "I hope this email finds you well", "I hope you're doing well" (unless the user actually writes like this)
- "I wanted to reach out", "I'm reaching out to..."
- Restating the question before answering it

Fix: start with the actual point, the ask, or the most interesting fact.

**Closers to cut**
- "In conclusion", "In summary", "To sum up", "Overall"
- "Ultimately, it's about...", "At the end of the day..."
- "By doing X, you can Y and Z" recap sentences
- "The future of X is bright / looks promising"
- "Remember, ..." moral at the end
- "I hope this helps!", "Feel free to reach out", "Don't hesitate to contact me"
- A question to the reader that nobody needs to answer ("What do you think?")

Fix: end on the last useful sentence. In emails, a plain sign-off with a concrete next step ("Can you send it by Friday?") is fine.

## 2. Sentence and paragraph shape

- **Uniform rhythm.** Every sentence 15-25 words, every paragraph 3-4 sentences. Vary it hard: a 4-word sentence next to a 30-word one.
- **Rule of three.** "fast, reliable, and secure"; "innovation, collaboration, and growth"; three bullet points every time. Use two, or four, or one. Use three only when there really are three things.
- **Paired adjectives / synonyms.** "clear and concise", "robust and scalable", "each and every". Pick one.
- **Every paragraph ends with a takeaway.** A neat moral line closing each section. Let some paragraphs just stop.
- **Topic sentence + elaboration + mini-summary** in every paragraph. Break the template.
- **-ing tails.** "..., highlighting the importance of X", "..., ensuring that Y", "..., reflecting a broader shift". Usually cut the tail or make it its own sentence with a real subject.
- **Perfect parallelism everywhere.** Lists where every item starts with the same verb form. Fine in instructions, odd in prose.

## 3. Rhetorical devices

One of these in a long piece can be a choice. Repeated, they're a fingerprint.

- **"It's not X, it's Y" / "This isn't just X, it's Y" / "not only X but also Y"** — the single strongest tell. Just say Y.
- **Colon or question reveal.** "The result? A 40% drop." "Here's the kicker:" "The secret:" Say it straight.
- **"Here's the thing", "Here's why", "Here's what you need to know"**
- **Rhetorical question answered immediately.** "So what does this mean? It means..."
- **Fake suspense.** "But there's a catch." "And that's where things get interesting."
- **"Think of it as / Imagine..."** analogies stacked one after another.
- **Antithesis lists.** "Less X, more Y. Less A, more B."
- **"Whether you're a beginner or an expert..."** universal-audience framing.

## 4. Tone problems

- **Over-hedging.** "may potentially", "it could be argued", "in some cases", "generally speaking", "it's worth noting", "it's important to remember". Keep a hedge only where the uncertainty is real.
- **Sycophancy.** "Great question!", "Absolutely!", "What a fantastic idea", "You're absolutely right".
- **Promo voice.** "game-changer", "revolutionary", "unlock your potential", "take X to the next level", "seamless experience", "cutting-edge". Describe what it does.
- **False balance.** "There are pros and cons", "It depends on your needs" with no actual recommendation. Pick a side or say what it depends on, concretely.
- **Vague significance.** "plays a crucial role", "is a testament to", "has a profound impact", "stands as a symbol of". Say what actually happened.
- **Weasel attribution.** "Experts say", "Studies show", "Many believe" with no source. Name it or drop it.
- **Emotional inflation.** "incredibly", "truly", "deeply", "profoundly" on everything.

## 5. Vocabulary

Words that are fine alone but cluster heavily in AI text. If you see two or three in one paragraph, the paragraph needs rewriting, not just word swaps.

**Verbs:** delve, embark, leverage, harness, foster, bolster, underscore, showcase, navigate (non-literal), unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, streamline, spearhead, illuminate, resonate, transcend, revolutionize, optimize, facilitate, utilize, endeavor, encompass, cultivate, amplify

**Nouns:** tapestry, landscape, realm, journey, testament, beacon, symphony, synergy, paradigm, intricacies, nuances, multifaceted nature, interplay, cornerstone, linchpin, treasure trove, game-changer, insights (overused), ecosystem (non-literal), framework (non-technical), stakeholders (casual contexts)

**Adjectives:** pivotal, crucial, vital, robust, seamless, comprehensive, holistic, dynamic, vibrant, bustling, intricate, nuanced, meticulous, profound, transformative, innovative, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, ever-evolving, fast-paced, unparalleled, invaluable, noteworthy, compelling, captivating, enchanting, breathtaking

**Adverbs and connectors:** furthermore, moreover, additionally, consequently, notably, importantly, essentially, ultimately, arguably, undeniably, seamlessly, meticulously, effortlessly; "that being said", "with that in mind", "in essence", "it's worth noting"

**Plain swaps (use when the word itself is the problem):**
| AI word | Try |
|---|---|
| utilize / leverage | use |
| facilitate | help, run, make easier |
| embark on | start |
| delve into | look at, dig into, get into |
| foster | build, encourage |
| robust | solid, strong, or cut |
| comprehensive | full, complete, or cut |
| crucial / pivotal / vital | important, key, or show why |
| furthermore / moreover | also, and, or nothing |
| navigate | handle, deal with, get through |
| showcase | show |
| numerous | many, lots of |
| in order to | to |
| prior to | before |

## 6. Punctuation and formatting

- **Em dashes (—).** The most-cited tell. Replace with commas, periods, parentheses or a colon. Default to none.
- **Semicolons** in casual writing. Use a period.
- **Title Case Headers** and bold mini-headers ("**Key takeaway:**") in short pieces.
- **Bullets for everything.** If it reads fine as two sentences, write two sentences.
- **Emoji bullets** (✅ 🚀 💡) unless the user's own posts use them.
- **Bold on random key phrases** throughout body text.
- **Curly quotes and fancy symbols** pasted into plain-text contexts (WhatsApp, SMS, code comments) can look copied. Match the destination.
- **Exclamation marks** on every other sentence, or none ever. Humans use them unevenly.

## 7. Chat-assistant residue

Things that leak from a chatbot answer into text the user will send.

- "Certainly! Here's a revised version:" left at the top
- "As an AI...", "I don't have personal experiences, but..."
- "[Your Name]", "[Company]" placeholders left unfilled (flag them instead)
- "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like any changes"
- Over-explaining obvious things to a reader who already knows them
- Answering a yes/no question with a 5-paragraph essay
- Knowledge-cutoff phrasing: "as of my last update"
